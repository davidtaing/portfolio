My boring answer is that I have always been fairly technical. I am the go-to IT support technician of the family, and I was one at the first real estate office I worked at.

The more interesting answer is a combination of a few things.

## I had been learning to program since 2014, and was a permanent beginner for a long time

I wrote my first line of code in 2014 and finished a Diploma of Software Development in 2015. But I never really put myself out there, and I did not work hard enough at it.

So I went from warehousing, then eventually into real estate for a few years.

Through all of that I was learning to program on and off. I would spend a few months programming, then stop, then start again, over and over.

Fast-forward to me as a property manager, in residential rentals.

## I built something that Dave the Property Manager would pay Dave the Software Developer for

At the end of each month I had a process that took me five hours. It involved scanning more than a hundred landlord statements, renaming each file, then emailing them manually, one by one.

I came across SendGrid and built a small application to batch send those emails from a spreadsheet.

It took the process down to one hour.

That is when it clicked. I finally understood what makes software engineering valuable.

> We are solving the problems of our customers.

I still carry that as an engineer.

## It is one of those rare careers where you can be paid well and be genuinely interested in the work

Paid well against passion. _Most people only get to pick one_.[^1]

With software engineering I get to pick both. That is a hell yes from me.

[^1]: I put that in italics because I think "follow your passion" is generally bad advice. That is a topic for another day. If you want to dig into it, I would recommend Cal Newport's *So Good They Can't Ignore You*.

## And finally, I believed I would be a much stronger engineer than I was a property manager

Frankly, I felt I would only ever be a mediocre property manager.

As an engineer, I believed I could be genuinely good. So good they can't ignore me.

So far I think that has proved true, and I am certainly heading in that direction.

## I went all in, and became a professional software engineer after 1,200 hours in VS Code

I am six months in and still early career.

I can see myself going deep as an individual contributor, going deep as an engineering manager, building my own startup, or working as a product manager.

Wherever I end up, I am excited to find out.
